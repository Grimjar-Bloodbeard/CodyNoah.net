The honest version

How I actually work

I don't read every line as the AI writes it - nobody does, at that speed. What I do instead: the code comes out clean and understandable every time, because that's how I've set the process up. So when something breaks - and things break - I can open it up, actually read it, and fix it myself.

Here's how it actually works: I run ideas through a few different AI models first - brainstorming, design, figuring out what's actually worth building and where to put my focus. Once I know what I'm building, Claude is my dedicated, paid developer - that's who does the dev work, most of the time. I'm also building my own local models to push the process further. I never stop looking for ways to improve it.

Straight truth: when I find something - a real security upgrade, a fix - I push it out to everyone I've built for, past clients included, free. Just because it's the right thing to do, and that's the standard I hold my work to. Outdated doesn't have to mean unsafe, not if you build with that in mind from the start.

About

I'm an independent developer and hardware tinkerer in Wilkes County, North Carolina - a decade in manufacturing, precision machining, and welding before I ever shipped a line of code. That background didn't get left at the door; it's why I build things meant to survive contact with the real world, not just look good in a demo. Self-taught, moving fast, and happiest with a problem in front of me.

Straight truth: the sites below, I built because I wanted to - not because anyone signed a check. A local game shop and a local senior center both run on things I made, and the education has been worth more to me than an invoice. That's starting to change. The habit of shipping real work isn't.

I forge with modern power tools - AI, automation, and a stubborn streak that doesn't know how to quit - and I'm not shy about it. The craft isn't pretending I hand-chiseled every line. It's knowing what to build, wiring it together clean, and making it survive contact with real users.

One more honest note: the forge is a metaphor, not a résumé line. Real years with metal - welding, machining - but I've never worked an actual smith's forge. I just like the aesthetic and the mindset enough to build a brand around it.
