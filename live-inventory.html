Live Inventory

One item, two places to sell it - the shop counter and the website. This keeps them in lockstep in real time: sell it in-store and the site updates in the same breath, sell it online and the register knows. Built for Summit Gaming of Wilkes; now being shaped into a product other small shops can use.

The problem

A shop that sells in two places has one recurring nightmare: the last copy of a game sells at the register and online in the same minute. Now it's oversold - someone gets a refund and an apology instead of their order. The common "fix" is manual: reconcile spreadsheets, refresh pages, hope. I didn't want two systems kept in sync by hand. I wanted one truth that updates every screen the instant it changes.

The engineering decisions

1. One source of truth, broadcast on change.

The database is the only thing that's ever "right." Every stock change commits there first, then fires an event. No screen ever guesses a count or polls for one - it gets told. That's what kills the drift.

2. Real-time over WebSockets, not refresh.

Socket.IO pushes the change the moment it lands, to every open screen at once - the counter, the manager's phone, a customer mid-checkout. Sub-second, no F5. That instant feel is the whole product.

3. A self-healing safety net.

If an event is ever missed - a dropped connection, a crash mid-write - a reconciliation pass catches it and corrects the count. A glitch stays a glitch; it never graduates into an oversold order. Failure was designed for, not discovered in production.

4. Thin client, heavy server.

Phone or desktop, the screen just listens. The real work stays server-side, so it runs great on a cheap tablet at the counter and an old laptop in the back office alike.

Where it's going - a SaaS for small shops

Today it runs one store. The core is being reshaped so any small retailer can have live inventory without enterprise pricing - one real-time engine, many shops, each walled off from the others.

The multi-tenant plan

  • Isolation at the data layer - every shop is scoped by a tenant key, enforced in one place so no shop can ever see another's numbers.
  • Real-time, per shop - each store gets its own broadcast room, so live updates only reach the people who should see them.
  • Its own front door - a subdomain per shop, their branding, their catalog.
  • Self-serve - sign up, import your catalog, go live - the parts that turn a system into a product.

Update: it's built - meet StockForge

The multi-tenant version is now a working product, StockForge: real-time stock synced across a register and a customer storefront, hard tenant isolation (one shop can never see another's numbers), a stampede test that proves it never oversells under a thousand simultaneous buyers, catalog upload, and AI photo-to-listing that keeps a human as the final say. Same engine as Summit, rebuilt for many shops.

Straight truth: it runs live at Summit today as a single shop, and StockForge is demo-ready but not yet publicly hosted. I'd rather show you exactly where the line is than oversell it.
