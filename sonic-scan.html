Sonic Scan

Snap one photo of a game. A vision model identifies it, proposes a price, and drafts the whole listing. A human reviews and hits publish. Built for Summit Gaming of Wilkes.

Computer visionHuman-in-the-loopPipeline designCost/latency aware

The problem

A retro-game shop lists dozens of items a week. Each one is photographed, identified, priced, described, and posted by hand - roughly ten minutes of tedium per item, and the bottleneck between "bought a collection" and "it's for sale." I wanted to compress that to a glance.

The pipeline

One photo in, a review-ready listing out. AI does the tedious 90%; a person keeps the final call.

The engineering decisions

1. AI interprets; code does the math.

The vision model decides what the item is - title, platform, edition. Deterministic code handles catalog matching and pricing. The model never touches money, so pricing stays predictable, auditable, and cheap.

2. Human-in-the-loop, by design.

The AI never publishes. It drafts; a person reviews and approves. That single gate turns "the model might be wrong" from a liability into a twenty-second check - and keeps a human accountable for what goes live.

3. Confidence and graceful failure.

A low-confidence identification gets flagged for a closer look instead of guessing. A bad photo degrades to "retake or fill in manually" - never a confidently wrong listing. Failure modes were designed on purpose, not discovered in production.

4. Fast where a human is waiting.

Capture and review are instant; the model call runs asynchronously so nobody stares at a spinner. Latency budget spent where it's felt, not where it isn't.

What I'd change now

The first version leaned on a cloud vision API for identification. A modern vision LLM - one that can look at a photo and reason about it - can do identify, describe, and draft in a single call: a simpler pipeline, and it can run locally on my own GPU for zero API cost and full privacy. That's the upgrade path, and it's exactly the local-first AI direction I'm building toward.

The result

~10 minutes  →  ~20 seconds per listing

Same catalog quality, a fraction of the effort - the difference between "I'll list it later" and "it's live."

What's protected

This is the shape of the system and the decisions behind it - not the recipe. The exact prompts, the pricing logic, and the catalog-matching stay under the hood. If you're evaluating me and want to go deeper on the engineering, I'm glad to walk through it live.
